Information Design Prototypes

My first area of exploration involved visual feedback for a music garden. Since the interface was designed for toddlers with limited language skills, I wanted the interactions to clearly convey information without relying on written instructions. Visual feedback should help children immediately understand the connection between their actions and the musical results as they interact with garden elements and hear sounds.

I focused on several aspects of the feedback design. First was visibility—was the visual feedback clear enough for toddlers to realize that their actions led to a certain result? Second was causality—did the feedback clearly establish the connection between pressing a garden element, seeing the visual feedback, and hearing the sound? I was also interested in fun, as the feedback should encourage children's curiosity and repeated exploration without distracting them or causing visual clutter. Since the same interaction might ultimately be applied to different garden elements, I also wanted to consider how the feedback could consistently convey interactive information across a wider interface.

To explore these possibilities, I developed three parallel prototypes, using scale feedback, glowing feedback, and musical note feedback, respectively. The prototypes maintained a consistent primary sound interaction method, while the visual responses varied. This allows each prototype to explore different interaction methods while remaining consistent with the project's values of "fun, curiosity, and ease of use."
